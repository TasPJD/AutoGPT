# Message and Discovery Library

All language is internal draft text. Paul must approve the actual recipient, facts, consent basis and send.

## Message construction rule

Every first contact contains only:

1. real relationship/context;
2. observed trigger or plausible capacity need;
3. one specific HarLin contribution;
4. one low-friction question;
5. no attachment unless useful and appropriate.

## Existing relationship — decision trigger

> Hi [Name] — as [context] moves toward [real decision/change], I wondered whether the difficult part is now less the raw technical work and more getting the evidence, assumptions and unresolved questions into one decision-ready view. I have developed a tightly bounded HarLin sprint for exactly that situation. If that problem is live, I can outline what a three-to-five-day scope would and would not cover. Is it worth a short conversation?

The model is designed to sit behind a consultancy’s client relationship, with your nominated specialist retaining final technical approval. I thought of [Firm] because [specific complement/trigger]. Would a 20-minute discussion about where deadline or specialist-capacity pressure actually arises be useful?

HarLin provides a rapid, bounded triage that maps the evidence, contradictions, material gaps and next questions for the decision owner. I’m not assuming that problem exists in your case; is there someone appropriate to ask whether it does?

## Follow-up 1 — value add, not a bump

> One useful distinction from our work: a document inventory answers what exists; a decision-evidence map also shows which claims depend on which sources, assumptions or missing approvals. That is usually where the material risk becomes visible. If [trigger] has made that relevant, I’m happy to send the one-page scope. If not, no action needed.

## Courteous close

> I’ll close the loop here so this doesn’t become inbox noise. If [specific problem] becomes live later, I would be glad to help or point you toward the right specialist. No reply is needed.

## Discovery structure — 25 minutes

### Opening

> I’m testing whether this is a real, purchasable problem. I’m more interested in the last time it occurred than whether the offer sounds attractive. I won’t treat this as authority to send anything further.

### Questions

1. What consequential decision or deliverable was most recently delayed by fragmented evidence?
2. What made it difficult: volume, contradictions, missing ownership, specialist language, confidence or deadline?
3. What did the organisation do instead, and what did that consume or put at risk?
4. Who felt the pain and who could authorise a solution?
5. What would a useful resolved state look like—memo, questions, evidence map, control register, board readout or something else?
6. What evidence could not leave the organisation, and what professional sign-off would be required?
7. What would make an external senior reviewer credible enough?
8. Would a bounded paid diagnostic be easier to approve than a broad engagement?
9. Which price band would trigger: immediate approval, formal procurement, or rejection?
10. What would cause the work to fail even if the analysis were good?

### Close

- Restate problem, consequence, owner and timing.
- Ask permission for one specific next action and date.
- Record objections verbatim.
- Do not improvise a price commitment outside the approved band.

## Qualification gate — DECIDE

Score 0 or 1. Qualified requires at least 4/6, with no zero on `D` or `E`.

- `D` Decision: a named consequential decision/deliverable exists.
- `E` Executive owner: someone owns the outcome and can sponsor action.
- `C` Cost: delay, uncertainty or rework has material consequence.
- `I` Inputs: a bounded evidence set can be accessed lawfully and safely.
- `D` Deadline: a real trigger creates timing.
- `E` Expertise: HarLin's proof and permitted scope fit the problem.

## Objection handling

| Objection | Response discipline |
|---|---|
| “We can use ChatGPT/Copilot internally.” | Agree that generation is not scarce; ask who validates sources, assumptions, permissions and acceptance for the consequential decision |
| “Our existing consultant should do this.” | Position HarLin as a bounded complement or independent challenge; never manufacture conflict |
| “We need certification/compliance.” | Clarify HarLin's evidence/gap role and introduce a qualified certification/legal/cyber partner where appropriate |
| “Too expensive.” | Reconfirm consequence and narrow scope; never lower price without reducing inputs, outputs, urgency or iterations |
| “Send information.” | Send the single relevant one-page scope and ask one dated question; do not begin a nurture sequence without consent |
| “No budget now.” | Ask what trigger/date changes that; park with a dated revisit or close cleanly |
